1. The problem

Eve's Space has a loyal base after six years, yet its chairs are 41% full and net profit fell from $5.5k in May to $590 in October. Marketing runs across Google, Instagram, influencers, ClassPass and Airbnb; revenue sits in Square, ClassPass and partner payouts. Nobody can answer the one question that matters: which marketing turns into profitable appointments, and what should I do this week?

2. The solution: an agent, not a dashboard

Evie is a single conversation. The owner opens it and reads what Evie already knows about the week; everything else is one message away. Evie connects channel → booking → margin per chair-hour and turns it into actions the owner approves with one tap. Nothing posts or spends without her yes.

Three things no salon tool does today
  • Gap Filler: marketing driven by inventory, not budget. For every open slot Evie picks the clients whose service and rhythm fit it and drafts a personal offer for that exact hour.
  • Monday Brief: a weekly letter from Evie: what happened, what she did, what she proposes, three taps to approve.
  • Reach-trap detector: came-in vs came-back per channel, so influencer spend that brings 77 clients and keeps 6 gets cut.

4. Expected business impact (the brief's metrics)

  • Profitable appointment growth: new-client bookings, rebook rate, occupancy, CAC and repeat visits tracked per channel; budget moves approved with their expected effect. Reaching $10k net needs ~190 more appointments a month (63% utilization, within capacity).
  • Consistent, authentic marketing: content in seconds, in the owner's voice, measured by booking codes.
  • Financial visibility: one merged P&L, margin by stream and service, a live goal tracker, no manual reconciliation.

6. Extra challenge: Brow Down Studio, same agent, zero new code

Brow Down runs three businesses in one: a brow/lash/facial studio, the Brow Down Studio Pro product line and education for pros. Campaign direction depends on the founder's creativity and availability (she wrote the entire Fall kit launch herself, 11 hours), and nobody knows whether Brow Down shows up when customers ask Google or an AI assistant.

We switched the studio and gave Evie Brow Down's data and voice. Three capabilities map directly to the brief:

  • Campaign Planner (insight → plan → execution): one line (“Holiday gift sets, ship by Dec 15, budget $600”) becomes a plan: 90 orders / $8,000 target, channel mix chosen from campaign learnings (“email drove 68% of Fall kit orders, TikTok 2%”), assets with owner (Founder / Content creator / Social media) and due dates, a 3-week calendar, copy drafted live and checked by a brand-voice guard. Approve plan → one approval per asset.
  • Launch retro, learning loop: marking a campaign done records result vs target and one learning; the next plan cites it.
  • Discovery: search + AI visibility: losing-rank alerts (“brow lamination kit” 9 → 14, 5,900 impressions) with content actions, and 12 customer questions sent live to a model to score whether Brow Down is mentioned (2–3 of 12 today). Misses become tasks: FAQ page, llms.txt + product schema, aftercare guide.

Day to day: René types the launch in one line, approves the plan, the team sees its assets and dates; every Monday the retro and the discovery score tell her what moved. Impact: launches start from evidence, preparation drops from hours to minutes, discoverability becomes a number the team can move.

7. How it is built

  • Enter (Converge.AI) end to end: React front end, Enter infrastructure for the database (every table carries studio_id), owner sign-in, storage, edge functions evie-ai (chat, studio, discovery modes; Gemini 3.5 Flash via Enter AI) and evie-image (ad visuals via Enter AI image model).
  • Demo-code gate for judges, shared hourly cap; no keys in the client; every outbound action requires an approval and is logged.
  • Connectors (Instagram, Gmail, Google Search Console / Analytics / Calendar, Framia) are linked in the Converge workspace by the owner; the app's data layer switches from demo to live per source.

8. Broader application

The channel → booking → margin model fits any capacity-limited, appointment-based business: salons, spas, barbers, fitness studios, clinics, tattoo studios, multi-location groups. Brow Down proved that a product + education brand needed only new data and two cards. The “SaaS is dead” version of this product is an agent paid for filled chair-hours, not a seat licence.
